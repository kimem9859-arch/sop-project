# Effects of Bounding-Box Looseness, Padding and Localization Noise in Labels on Detector Training and Evaluation (small objects, YOLO-family)

Scope note: every number below is quoted with its experimental condition. Where a number was read from the paper's own PDF text (pdftotext) rather than a web summary, that is the source of truth; one early web-summary extraction (UNA, ShrinkBox) was found to be wrong and was replaced by the PDF text. Author inferences and my own arithmetic are kept in the "Inferences" subsections.

## Q1. Random box-coordinate noise in training labels: measured mAP effects, mAP50 vs mAP50-95

### Takeaway
Random (zero-mean) box jitter in training labels hurts strict-IoU metrics much more than AP50: on COCO with Faster R-CNN, ±20% uniform jitter of centre/size costs about 6.5% of AP50 (relative) but about 20% of AP (0.5:0.95) and 23% of AP75. Small jitter levels (≤10% of box size) cost very little (≤1 AP point at AP50 on VOC, <1 AP on COCO under UNA's milder scheme). Localization noise is second only to class noise in harm among single noise types (UNA).

### Cited Findings
- **OA-MIL noise model (widely reused):** ĉx = cx + Δx·w, ĉy = cy + Δy·h, ŵ = (1+Δw)·w, ĥ = (1+Δh)·h, with Δx, Δy, Δw, Δh ~ U(−r, r); r = "box noise level" (10%–40%). Applied to PASCAL VOC 2007 and MS-COCO training boxes. — [Liu et al., Robust Object Detection With Inaccurate Bounding Boxes, ECCV 2022 (arXiv 2207.09697)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09697)
- **VOC 2007, Faster R-CNN, AP50 (mAP@0.5):** clean 77.2; noise 10% → 76.3; 20% → 71.2; 30% → 60.1; 40% → 42.5 ("Noisy-FasterRCNN" baseline). — [Liu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09697) (also in [ar5iv HTML](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.09697))
- **MS-COCO, Faster R-CNN (Table 2, read from PDF text):** clean AP 37.9 / AP50 58.1 / AP75 40.9 / APS 21.6 / APM 41.6 / APL 48.7. At 20% box noise: AP 30.4 / AP50 54.3 / AP75 31.4 / APS 17.4 / APM 33.9 / APL 38.7. At 40% box noise: AP 10.3 / AP50 28.9 / AP75 3.3 / APS 5.7 / APM 11.8 / APL 15.1. Authors: "under 40% box noise, the vanilla model suffers from catastrophic performance drop, e.g., AP50 drops from 58.1 to 28.9." — [Liu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09697)
- **Li et al. 2020 noise model:** "we perturb the coordinates of all bounding boxes by a number of pixels uniformly drawn from [−wNb%, +wNb%] (w is bbox width) for horizontal coordinates or [−hNb%, +hNb%] (h is bbox height) for vertical coordinates." "Under 40% bbox noise, the average IoU between a noisy bbox and its corresponding clean bbox is only 0.45." — [Li, Xiong, Socher, Hoi, Towards Noise-resistant Object Detection with Noisy Annotations (arXiv 2003.01285)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.01285)
- **Li et al. 2020, VOC07 test mAP@.5, Faster R-CNN R50-FPN (vanilla), no label noise:** 75.5 at 20% bbox noise, 59.3 at 40% bbox noise (with 20%/40% class-label noise added: 70.7/66.9 at 20% bbox noise; 54.2/50.0 at 40% bbox noise). — [Li et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.01285)
- **UNA benchmark (COCO, Faster R-CNN R50-FPN, 12 epochs, metric AP; Table 2 read from PDF text):** clean 37.4. Localization noise: 5% → 37.0 (−0.4); 10% → 36.6 (−0.8); 15% → 35.7 (−1.7); 20% → 35.4 (−2.0). At 20%: categorization noise 35.0 (−2.4), missing annotations 36.1 (−1.3), bogus boxes 36.5 (−0.9), all four combined ("UNA") 26.6 (−10.8). — [UNA, Universal Noise Annotation (arXiv 2312.13822)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.13822)
- UNA authors state that "Previous attempts [27, 23] to simulate localization noise in object detection often resulted in overly strong noise. Therefore, we introduce new noise generation scheme" (details only in supplementary material) and rank impact: "categorization noise had the most significant negative impact on performance, followed by localization noise, missing annotation noise, and bogus bounding box noise." — [UNA (arXiv 2312.13822)](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.13822)
- OA-MIL authors: "localization precision suffers significantly from inaccurate bounding boxes while classification accuracy is less affected." — [Liu et al. 2022 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.09697)
- "Beyond clean data" (Knowledge-Based Systems vol. 304, Oct 2024) studies noisy labels with YOLOv5, YOLOv8 and Faster R-CNN on MS COCO, VOC, ExDARK (plus a dense UVM dataset) and uses D-RISE saliency maps; full text is paywalled (ScienceDirect returned HTTP 403), so no numbers could be extracted. — [ACM DL listing](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1016/j.knosys.2024.112544); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S095070512401178X)
- Ultralytics definitions: mAP50 = "Mean average precision calculated at an intersection over union (IoU) threshold of 0.50 ... considering only the 'easy' detections"; mAP50-95 = "The average of the mean average precision calculated at varying IoU thresholds, ranging from 0.50 to 0.95." — [Ultralytics YOLO performance metrics guide](https://docs.ultralytics.com/guides/yolo-performance-metrics/)

### Inferences
- Relative drops computed from OA-MIL COCO Table 2 at 20% noise: AP50 −6.5% (58.1→54.3), AP −19.8% (37.9→30.4), AP75 −23.2% (40.9→31.4), APS −19.4% (21.6→17.4). So random label jitter mostly shows up above IoU 0.5, i.e., in mAP50-95, not mAP50. (my arithmetic)
- At r = 10% on VOC the AP50 cost was only 0.9 points; under UNA's milder scheme 10% noise cost 0.8 AP on COCO. This suggests that annotator jitter on the order of a few percent of box size is not a first-order problem for AP50, but will be visible in mAP50-95. (inference)
- All controlled studies found used Faster R-CNN / RetinaNet-class detectors on COCO/VOC; no controlled YOLOv8 localization-noise sweep with numbers was obtainable (the only one, "Beyond clean data", is paywalled). Transfer to YOLOv8n is an assumption.

### Gaps
- UNA's exact localization-noise generator and what "5%–20%" means (fraction of boxes vs magnitude) are only in the supplementary, not retrieved.
- No accessible numbers for YOLOv5/YOLOv8 under box noise ("Beyond clean data" paywalled).
- None of the studies reported mAP50 vs mAP50-95 separately *for small objects with few-pixel jitter*; OA-MIL reports APS only for whole-dataset noise levels.

## Q2. Systematic bias: all boxes consistently larger or smaller (padding / shrinking)

### Takeaway
I found no controlled paper that pads/shrinks *all* training boxes by a fixed pixel or percent margin and reports mAP50 vs mAP50-95 against tight test labels. The nearest evidence shows that (i) detectors learn and reproduce a consistent box-size convention, (ii) mAP measured against labels with the same convention cannot reveal it, (iii) box size can act as a free hyper-parameter trading mAP against point-localization error, and (iv) humans actually prefer boxes larger than tight GT.

### Cited Findings
- **ShrinkBox (backdoor, KITTI, YOLOv9/YOLOv10):** poisoned instances get boxes shrunk by a height-dependent amount (chosen so a downstream distance estimator reads the object as +5 m farther) plus a trigger patch; 60 models trained (6 clean, 54 poisoned at 9 poisoning ratios). Verbatim: "The effectiveness of the ShrinkBox attack cannot be evaluated using the detector's mAP, as the attack does not alter mAP. This limitation arises because, even under a successful attack, the shrunken predicted bounding boxes align with the [shrunken ground truth]". YOLOv9m reached ASR 96.4% "with a negligible difference between mAPbenign and mAPpoison", while downstream distance MAE on poisoned instances rose 3.3× (1.67 m → 5.51 m). Training: 640×200 input, 200 epochs, mosaic + flip. — [ShrinkBox (arXiv 2507.18656)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18656)
- **Box size as a hyper-parameter for point-like targets (YOLOv7, BDD100K, pole bases encoded as fixed-size square boxes centred on the point; conf 0.25; 100 epochs):** mAP0.5:0.95 / precision / recall / horizontal centre MAE: 60×60: 42.5 / 71.8% / 58.7% / 2.28 px; 80×80: 44.8 / 74.2% / 55.0% / 2.87 px; 100×100: 54.7 / 67.6% / 68.1% / 3.18 px; 150×150: 57.6 / 76.5% / 61.8% / 4.23 px; 200×200: 58.2 / 76.7% / 61.9% / 5.29 px; 250×250: 58.5 / 66.1% / 65.6% / 7.90 px; 300×300: 55.6 / 71.9% / 63.7% / 9.73 px; 350×350: 55.2 / 67.7% / 61.2% / 12.5 px; 400×400: 60.4 / 73.2% / 62.8% / 15.8 px. Authors: "enlarging the box size introduces more uncertainty in the pole base position ... Significantly increasing box size can enhance overall performance ... But, it is imperative to note a substantial increase of MAE." "The box size consequently become a new hyperparameter." — [Map-aided annotation for pole base detection (arXiv 2403.01868)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01868)
- **Human preference for larger boxes:** "Previous work shows that humans tend to prefer large bounding boxes over small bounding boxes with the same IoU. However, we show here that commonly used object detectors predict large and small boxes equally often." User study N = 123: "humans prefer object detections that are upscaled with factors of 1.5 or 2, even if the corresponding AP is close to 0." Standard regression loss "is symmetric for errors in bounding box size: a predicted box that is 10% too large will give the same loss as a box that is 10% too small." For IoU ∈ [0.6, 0.7], "where humans would choose a large box over a small box with approximately 70% chance, an object detector [does so] with ... equal probability." (Faster R-CNN, RetinaNet, Cascade Mask R-CNN on COCO val.) — [Aligning Object Detector Bounding Boxes with Human Preference (arXiv 2408.10844)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.10844)
- Real annotation data contain systematic, not just random, deviation: in VOC 2012, comparing human boxes (AHBB) to boxes derived from segmentation masks (SHBB), only 73.46% of top-left offsets satisfy Δx_tl ≤ 0 and Δy_tl ≤ 0, 88.28% of bottom-right offsets satisfy Δx_br ≥ 0 and Δy_br ≥ 0, and only 66.09% satisfy all four "bounding" constraints; ~95% of offsets lie within [−16, 16]² px. — [Murrugarra-Llerena et al., Can We Trust Bounding Box Annotations for Object Detection?, CVPRW 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- Community opinion (not an experiment): "as long as you do not have systematic bias (all your box a much bigger than objects, or all your box are left offset, etc), the noise in your annotation does not matter much" — user *healthmatrice* (community member, not an Ultralytics maintainer), who adds that tighter boxes help "to a degree" and to check "what is the percentage of the offset error". — [ultralytics/yolov5 Discussion #10813](https://github.com/ultralytics/yolov5/discussions/10813)

### Inferences
- Analytic IoU of a tight box vs the same box padded by p (fraction of width) on every side: IoU = 1/(1+2p)². p = 2% → 0.925; 5% → 0.826; 10% → 0.694; 15% → 0.592; 20% → 0.51. So if the model faithfully learns a padded convention and is scored against tight labels (or vice versa), mAP50 is essentially unaffected up to ~20% per-side padding, but every IoU threshold above 1/(1+2p)² is lost; a 10%/side pad already fails thresholds 0.70–0.95 (6 of the 10 mAP50-95 thresholds). (my arithmetic)
- Consistency (same convention in train and test) makes a systematic bias invisible to mAP (ShrinkBox); therefore mAP cannot be used to choose between "tight" and "padded" — the choice must be justified by the downstream use (here: fingertip-inside-box judgement), exactly as the pole-base paper chose box size by downstream precision/MAE. (inference)
- The human-preference result implies human annotators and reviewers may drift toward looser boxes unless the rule is explicit. (inference)

### Gaps
- No study found that trains YOLO (any version) on uniformly padded vs tight labels of the *same* objects and evaluates both on tight and padded test labels.
- No study found on whether a detector trained on padded labels predicts correspondingly padded boxes for *small* objects (ShrinkBox shows it for cars in KITTI with a trigger, not a global convention).

## Q3. Small objects: how few pixels of box error change IoU (20–100 px), COCO definition, IoU over-sensitivity

### Takeaway
IoU error from a fixed pixel offset scales with 1/size: a 1 px diagonal shift gives IoU 0.53 on a 6×6 px object but 0.90 on a 36×36 px object; even sub-pixel rounding measurably lowers AP75/AP95 for COCO small objects. For 19–94 px boxes, 2–5 px of error (a typical human error is ~5 px per side per ILSVRC) moves IoU into the 0.5–0.85 band, which is exactly the range mAP50-95 thresholds sample.

### Cited Findings
- **COCO size buckets:** small / medium / large objects "are separated by area thresholds of 32² and 96² as in the official evaluation tools of COCO." — [Murrugarra-Llerena et al. CVPRW 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- **NWD paper:** "IoU ... and its extensions are very sensitive to the location deviation of the tiny objects." "For the tiny object with 6 × 6 pixels, a minor location deviation will lead to notable IoU drop (from 0.53 to 0.06) ... for the normal object with 36 × 36 pixels, the IoU changes slightly (from 0.90 to 0.65) with the same location deviation" (Fig. 1: 1 px and 4 px diagonal deviation). Tiny objects in AI-TOD are "less than 16 × 16 pixels". Also: with IoU-based assignment the average number of positive samples per GT in AI-TOD is below one. — [Wang et al., A Normalized Gaussian Wasserstein Distance for Tiny Object Detection (arXiv 2110.13389)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.13389)
- **ILSVRC loosened threshold for small objects:** "for small objects even deviations of a few pixels would be unacceptable according to this threshold. For example, consider an object B of size 10 × 10 pixels, with a detection window of 20 × 20 pixels which fully contains that object. This would be an error of approximately 5 pixels on each dimension, which is average human annotation error. However, the IOU in this case would be 100/400 = 0.25." ILSVRC therefore uses thr(B) = min(0.5, wh/((w+10)(h+10))) "to allow for the annotation to extend up to 5 pixels on average in each direction"; "this changes the threshold only on objects which are smaller than approximately 25 × 25 pixels, and affects 5.5% of objects in the detection validation set." — [Russakovsky et al., ImageNet Large Scale Visual Recognition Challenge, IJCV 2015 (arXiv 1409.0575)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0575)
- **Sub-pixel rounding (COCO):** rounding COCO's float mask-derived boxes to integers (floor top-left, ceil bottom-right; per-coordinate error < 1 px) — IoU vs smallest box dimension has Spearman ρ = 0.9367; lower bound of IoU for minimum dimension d is d²/(d+2)². Categories most affected: car, traffic light, bird, sports ball, book — "the underlying reason is not the category itself, but the size of the HBBs." — [Murrugarra-Llerena et al. CVPRW 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- **Ideal detector (predicts the float boxes exactly) scored against the integer-rounded boxes, COCO val, small objects:** AP50 99.60, AP75 93.46, AP95 13.63, AP50:95 82.38 (vs 100 for all under matching convention). "The ideal detector is not affected by sub-pixel discrepancies at all IoU levels for large objects. However, for medium and particularly for small objects, there is a strong IoU decrease for larger values of T." — [same](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- **Real detectors (trained on float boxes), COCO small objects, scored float / rounded:** EfficientDet D0 AP50:95 12.53 / 12.86; EfficientDet D7 36.92 / 36.36; YOLO-R P6 37.04 / 36.22 (AP95 2.48 / 1.60); YOLO-R W6 37.92 / 36.96. "Yolo-R W6 presents the best AP50:95 results using SHBB annotations for small objects, but is only the third-best when considering AHBBs." — [same](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- Under OA-MIL box noise on COCO, APS fell 21.6 → 17.4 (20% noise) and → 5.7 (40% noise). — [Liu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.09697)

### Inferences
- My computed IoU for square boxes of width w with error k px (GT vs perturbed box):

| w (px) | 1-axis shift k=2 | diag shift k=2 | pad k=1/side | pad k=2/side | pad k=3/side | pad k=5/side | shrink k=2/side |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 19 | 0.810 | 0.667 | 0.819 | 0.682 | 0.578 | 0.429 | 0.623 |
| 32 | 0.882 | 0.784 | 0.886 | 0.790 | 0.709 | 0.580 | 0.766 |
| 50 | 0.923 | 0.855 | 0.925 | 0.857 | 0.797 | 0.694 | 0.846 |
| 60 | 0.935 | 0.877 | 0.937 | 0.879 | 0.826 | 0.735 | 0.871 |
| 94 | 0.958 | 0.919 | 0.959 | 0.920 | 0.884 | 0.817 | 0.917 |

- Per-side outward padding (px) at which a tight-vs-padded pair falls below an IoU threshold: IoU 0.95 → 0.25 px (w=19), 0.65 px (w=50), 0.78 px (w=60), 1.2 px (w=94); IoU 0.75 → 1.5 px (19), 3.9 px (50), 4.6 px (60), 7.3 px (94); IoU 0.5 → 3.9 px (19), 10.4 px (50), 12.4 px (60), 19.5 px (94). (my arithmetic, IoU = w²/(w+2k)²)
- ILSVRC formula applied to square boxes: thr = 0.43 at 19 px, 0.44 at 20 px, 0.50 at ≥25 px — i.e., ILSVRC would already have loosened the IoU test for the smallest (~19 px) buttons. (my arithmetic)
- Scale invariance: a 1 px/side error on a 16 px box equals a 2 px/side error on a 32 px box (both IoU 0.79), so pixel tolerances must be stated relative to box size. (my arithmetic)
- Most 19–94 px boxes are COCO "small" (<32² = 1024 px² area) or "medium" by area, the regime where the CVPRW 2022 paper found sub-pixel sensitivity. (inference)

### Gaps
- No empirical study found specifically on JPEG-compressed, motion-blurred small objects and how far human box edges scatter on them.

## Q4. Human inter-annotator variability of boxes; what IoU counts as "the same box"

### Takeaway
Expert human boxes of the same object agree at about 0.88 mean IoU on PASCAL VOC (≈ perturbing each coordinate by 4 px), with ~93% of pairs above IoU 0.7; ILSVRC treats ~5 px per side as "average human annotation error" and counts a box as acceptable if visibly tight (none below IoU 0.5). Human-vs-human agreement expressed as mAP@[.5:.95] is only ~65 on LVIS even under the same guideline, and far lower (~20–28) when two different guidelines are compared.

### Cited Findings
- **Extreme clicking paper (PASCAL VOC 2007):** "Comparing GT Boxes and GT SegBoxes reveals this agreement to be at 88% mIoU on VOC 2007. Moreover, 93% of all GT Boxes have IoU > 0.7 with their corresponding GT SegBox ... Similar findings apply to VOC 2012." "If we perturb each of the four coordinates of the GT Boxes by 4 pixels, the resulting boxes also have 88% mIoU with the unperturbed annotations." Extreme-click boxes: mIoU 88, 92% IoU>0.7, 98% IoU>0.5; PASCAL GT boxes: 88 / 93 / 98; Fast R-CNN trained on either gives the same mAP (56 AlexNet, 66 VGG16); annotation time 7.0 s vs 34.5 s per instance. Crowd quality: qualification test raised mIoU from 75.4% to 85.7%, quality control to 87.1%. — [Papadopoulos et al., Extreme Clicking for Efficient Object Annotation, ICCV 2017](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2017/papers/Papadopoulos_Extreme_Clicking_for_ICCV_2017_paper.pdf)
- **ILSVRC box quality:** "On the bounding box level, 99.2% of all bounding boxes are accurate (the bounding boxes are visibly tight). The remaining 0.8% are somewhat off. No bounding boxes are found to have less than 50% intersection over union overlap with ground truth." Missing boxes were "all difficult cases: the size is too small, the boundary is blurry, or there is strong shadow." ~5 px per dimension is called "average human annotation error". — [Russakovsky et al. IJCV 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0575)
- **VOC human boxes vs mask-derived boxes (6,909 matched pairs):** median IoU for "bottle" is below 0.8 and ~15% of bottle samples have IoU < 0.5, so an ideal detector would have an AP50 upper bound of ~85% for that class; occluded objects are boxed inconsistently (sometimes only the visible core, sometimes guessed hidden extent); Spearman ρ between IoU and smallest box dimension = 0.79. — [Murrugarra-Llerena et al. CVPRW 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- **Label convergence (annotator-vs-annotator mAP, bootstrapped 1,000×, 10% samples):** LVIS consistency subset (same guideline, 2 raters/img) detection mAP@[0.5:0.95:0.05] mean 65.08, 95% CI 62.64–67.52 (SOTA 66.8); segmentation 59.78 (57.32–62.24). COCO re-annotated vs original (different guideline, 5 classes) 28.39 (25.35–31.43); Open Images re-annotated (different guideline) 20.55 (17.33–23.75). The COCO/Open Images pairs were excluded from the convergence estimate because "The datasets did not adhere to the same guidelines ... resulting in label conventions that deviate significantly." Fitted relation mAP = 0.836·K-α + 0.197. — [Label Convergence (arXiv 2409.09412)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.09412)
- Common acceptance thresholds used in evaluation: KITTI-style per-category thresholds "0.7 for cars, and 0.5 for pedestrians"; PASCAL VOC 0.5; COCO 0.50–0.95. — [Murrugarra-Llerena et al. CVPRW 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)

### Inferences
- "Same box" in practice: IoU ≥ 0.7 is the level at which ~93% of expert re-draws agree on VOC (mostly large objects); mean ≈ 0.88. For a 50–60 px box, IoU 0.88 corresponds to only ~1.5–2 px outward error per side (from the Q3 table), so human re-draw scatter on our button sizes plausibly sits right at the 0.8–0.9 band. (inference)
- Because human-human agreement caps achievable mAP50-95 (≈65 on LVIS), mAP50-95 differences of a few points between labeling conventions may be within annotator noise; mAP50 is more stable. (inference)

### Gaps
- Su, Deng & Fei-Fei 2012 (ImageNet crowd box quality, IoU 0.7 criterion) is pre-2015 and was not retrieved; COCO's own crowd-box agreement numbers were not found.
- No inter-annotator numbers found specifically for objects of 20–100 px or for blurred objects.

## Q5. Train/test tightness-convention mismatch, and consistency vs exact tightness

### Takeaway
A convention mismatch between the labels a detector learns from and the labels it is scored against lowers AP even for a perfect detector, the loss grows sharply with IoU threshold and with smaller object size, and it can re-order models. Conversely, a consistent convention in both sets hides any systematic bias from mAP. The measured size of the mismatch effect (≈4–9% of AP50:95 on VOC) is larger than the gains of many published box-loss improvements.

### Cited Findings
- **VOC: trained on human boxes, validated on human vs mask-derived boxes:** ideal detector: "even using T = 0.5 produces a 4% AP50 reduction ... and the degradation is over 22% when considering the COCO AP metric." EfficientDet D0 and SSD300 (ResNet50), trained on VOC07 human boxes: AP50 drop ~2%; AP75 drop 4.5% (EfficientDet) and 6.3% (SSD300); AP50:95 drop 7.6% and 9.3%. "Distance-IoU reports an AP75 of 56.34% ... vs. 54.74% using the IoU loss ... a 1.6% improvement. Such gain is considerably smaller than the 6.3% gap shown in our experiments by just changing the GT annotation." "disparities between the SHBB and AHBB can harm or benefit the performance of detectors." — [Murrugarra-Llerena et al. CVPRW 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- **DOTA/iSAID OBBs (annotated vs mask-derived):** ideal detector "almost 11% AP50 accuracy drop", AP75 48.9%, AP95 5.6%, AP50:95 51.6%; RetinaNet and R3Det AP50 drops ~12% and ~9%. — [same](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- **Model ranking can flip on small objects** under a sub-pixel convention change (YOLO-R W6 best → third). — [same](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022W/VDU/papers/Murrugarra-Llerena_Can_We_Trust_Bounding_Box_Annotations_for_Object_Detection_CVPRW_2022_paper.pdf)
- **Consistent bias is invisible to mAP:** ShrinkBox models show negligible mAPbenign vs mAPpoison difference because shrunken predictions match shrunken labels. — [ShrinkBox (arXiv 2507.18656)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.18656)
- **Different guidelines ≫ same-guideline noise:** annotator-vs-annotator mAP@[.5:.95] ≈ 65 under one guideline (LVIS) vs ≈ 20–28 across different guidelines (Open Images / COCO re-annotations). — [Label Convergence (arXiv 2409.09412)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.09412)
- **Practitioner guidance (course material, no experiment cited):** "Tight boxes / masks. No slack — labels must closely enclose each object, with no space between an object and its bounding box." "Good labels beat almost every other improvement. A model trained on consistent, tight labels and 1500 images per class will outperform a model trained on sloppy labels and 5000." "If one forklift includes its forks and the next excludes them, the model gets mixed signals." "Pick one rule (e.g. 'label if ≥ 50% visible') and stick to it." — [Ultralytics Academy, Annotation Best Practices](https://academy.ultralytics.com/courses/dataset-readiness-for-yolo/annotation-best-practices)

### Inferences
- If train labels are padded and test labels are tight (or vice versa), expect mAP50 nearly unchanged but mAP50-95 depressed on small buttons, and the model may look worse than it is; if both are padded, mAP will look normal but predicted boxes will be systematically larger, which directly enlarges the "fingertip inside box" region (area × (1+2p)²). (inference, combining Q2/Q3 arithmetic with the cited mismatch results)
- The evidence supports: pick one explicit edge rule, apply it identically to train, val and test (including any sealed test set), and document it; the specific choice (tight vs +k px) should be decided by the downstream touch-judgement tolerance, not by mAP. (inference)
- The Ultralytics "1500 vs 5000" claim is an unsupported assertion in teaching material and should not be cited as measured evidence. (source evaluation)

### Gaps
- No controlled experiment found that isolates "consistent but loose" vs "inconsistent but on average tight" labels with a YOLO detector.

## Q6. Blurry edges / motion blur: where to place the box edge

### Takeaway
I found no peer-reviewed study or major-dataset rule that specifies where to put a box edge on a motion-blurred object. The only primary-source evidence is that blurred boundaries are a known source of annotation failure (ILSVRC) and that major guidelines box the *visible* extent; everything more specific comes from vendor blogs.

### Cited Findings
- ILSVRC quality audit: the missing boxes were "all difficult cases: the size is too small, the boundary is blurry, or there is strong shadow." — [Russakovsky et al. IJCV 2015](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.0575)
- Ultralytics Academy: "When a forklift is half-hidden behind a rack, box the visible part — not where you imagine the rest continues." "Boxes must hug the object." (occlusion rule; the page does not address motion blur or small objects). — [Ultralytics Academy, Annotation Best Practices](https://academy.ultralytics.com/courses/dataset-readiness-for-yolo/annotation-best-practices)
- MOT16 benchmark paper only notes that its night sequence "contains a lot of motion blur, making pedestrian detection and tracking rather challenging"; no box-edge rule for blur was found in the text. — [Milan et al., MOT16 (arXiv 1603.00831)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.00831)
- Vendor/blog pages (low-quality sources, search-snippet level only) say to box "the visible portion of the object, even when motion blur is present" and that edges "should touch the outermost pixels of the object" — [Sama blog](https://www.sama.com/blog/bounding-box-computer-vision); [CNTXT AI](https://www.cntxt.tech/insights/annotating-with-bounding-boxes-quality-best-practices); [Label Your Data](https://labelyourdata.com/articles/data-annotation/bounding-box-annotation) (the Label Your Data page, when fetched, contained no blur or padding guidance).

### Inferences
- A blurred edge is a band several pixels wide; whether the annotator puts the edge at the outer end of the smear, the midpoint, or the sharp core is an arbitrary convention. If annotators vary, that variance behaves like random localization noise (Q1: mainly hurts mAP50-95); if one rule is applied consistently, it behaves like a systematic bias (Q2: invisible to mAP, but shifts predicted box size). For a 50–60 px box, a 3 px/side difference between "outer smear" and "core" already gives IoU ≈ 0.80–0.83 (Q3 table). (inference)
- Given the PASCAL-style "visible extent" norm and the absence of evidence either way, the defensible move is an explicit written rule (e.g., "edge at the mid-point of the blur transition" or "outer limit of the visible smear") applied identically to train and test. (inference)

### Gaps
- No study quantifying annotator scatter or detector effects on motion-blurred objects was found.
- PASCAL VOC 2007 annotation guidelines page (visible-extent / few-pixel exclusion rule) could not be fetched (connection refused), so its exact wording is not cited here.
