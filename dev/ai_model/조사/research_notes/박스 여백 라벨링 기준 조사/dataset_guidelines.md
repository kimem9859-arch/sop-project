# What official annotation guidelines of major public object-detection datasets say about bounding-box tightness (outermost visible pixels vs. margin) and hard edge cases

> Method note: every quote below was pulled from the primary document itself (PDF downloaded and converted with `pdftotext`, or the official HTML/Markdown fetched directly) on 2026-09-28, except BDD100K's instruction page, which was unreachable (DNS failure) and is quoted from search-engine snippets of the official page. That is marked **[secondary: search snippet]**. Quotes are verbatim. Words in [brackets] are mine.

## 1. PASCAL VOC (2007–2012): box extent, occlusion, truncation, "difficult"

### Takeaway
VOC defines the box as the **visible extent only**: *"Bounding box should contain all visible pixels"*. There is one explicit exception: a box does not have to be made "excessively large" to take in a few thin protruding pixels (<5%, e.g. a car aerial). VOC never mentions a margin or padding. Occlusion and truncation are handled with flags (Truncated, Occluded, Difficult), not by estimating the hidden extent. The 50% IoU evaluation threshold was set low on purpose, to tolerate ground-truth box inaccuracy.

### Cited Findings
- VOC2011/2012 guidelines, "Bounding box": *"Mark the bounding box of the visible area of the object (not the estimated total extent of the object)."* and *"Bounding box should contain all visible pixels, except where the bounding box would have to be made excessively large to include a few additional pixels (<5%) e.g. a car aerial."* — [VOC2012 Annotation Guidelines (page titled "VOC2011 Annotation Guidelines")](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- VOC2007 uses the same two sentences word for word — [VOC2007 Annotation Guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2007/guidelines.html)
- What not to label (2011/12): *"All objects of the defined categories, unless: you are unsure what the object is. the object is very small (at your discretion). less than 10-20% of the object is visible, such that you cannot be sure what class it is. e.g. if only a tyre is visible it may belong to car or truck so cannot be labelled car, but feet/faces can only belong to a person."* — [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html). VOC2007 has the shorter form *"less than 10-20% of the object is visible."* — [VOC2007 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2007/guidelines.html)
- Truncation (2011/12): *"If more than 15-20% of the object lies outside the bounding box mark as Truncated. The flag indicates that the bounding box does not cover the total extent of the object."* — [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- Occlusion (2011/12): *"If more than 5% of the object is occluded within the bounding box, mark as Occluded. The flag indicates that the object is not totally visible within the bounding box."* — [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- VOC2007 used a single combined rule: *"If more than 15-20% of the object is occluded and lies outside the bounding box, mark as Truncated. Do not mark as truncated if the occluded area lies within the bounding box."* — [VOC2007 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2007/guidelines.html)
- Close-fitting occluders: *"If an object is 'occluded' by a close-fitting occluder e.g. clothing, mud, snow etc., then the occluder should be treated as part of the object."* — [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- Image quality: *"Images which are poor quality (e.g. excessive motion blur) should be marked bad. However, poor illumination (e.g. objects in silhouette) should not count as poor quality unless objects cannot be recognised."* — [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- Transparency and mirrors: *"Do label objects visible through glass, but treat reflections on the glass as occlusion."* and *"Do label objects in mirrors."* — [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- The segmentation rules in the same document give the only explicit VOC rule on blurred edges: *"Segment within 5 pixels. Labelled pixels MUST be the object; pixels outside the 5-pixel border area MUST be background. Border pixels can be either."* and *"Pixels which are mixed e.g. due to transparency, motion blur or the presence of a border should be considered to belong to the object whose colour contributes most to the mix."* Also: *"Structures of around one pixel thickness can be ignored e.g. wires, rigging, whiskers."* — [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- Devkit attribute definitions: *"bounding box: an axis-aligned rectangle specifying the extent of the object visible in the image."*; *"'truncated': ... the bounding box specified for the object does not correspond to the full extent of the object e.g. an image of a person from the waist up, or a view of a car extending outside the image."*; *"'occluded': ... a significant portion of the object within the bounding box is occluded by another object."*; *"'difficult': ... the object is considered difficult to recognize, for example an object which is clearly visible but unidentifiable without substantial use of context. Objects marked as difficult are currently ignored in the evaluation of the challenge."* — [VOC2012 devkit doc](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/devkit_doc.pdf)
- IJCV paper, "difficult": *"labels objects which are particularly difficult to detect due to small size, illumination, image quality or the need to use significant contextual information. In the challenge evaluation, such objects are discarded, although no penalty is incurred for detecting them."* Criteria: *"confidence in the class label ... object size, level of occlusion, imaging factors e.g. motion blur, and requirement for significant context to enable recognition."* — [Everingham et al., IJCV 2010](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/pubs/everingham10.pdf)
- IJCV paper on box choice: *"an axis-aligned bounding box surrounding the extent of the object visible in the image."*; it calls the axis-aligned box *"a compromise"* that can be *"a poor fit"* for non-box-shaped or non-axis-aligned objects — [Everingham et al., IJCV 2010](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/pubs/everingham10.pdf)
- Annotation process: *"all annotation take place at a single annotation 'party' at the University of Leeds, following a set of annotation guidelines ... annotators were periodically observed to ensure that the guidelines were being followed."*; *"the accuracy of each annotation was checked by one of the organisers"*. No numeric box-agreement figure is given — [Everingham et al., IJCV 2010](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/pubs/everingham10.pdf)
- Evaluation tolerance: *"The threshold of 50% was set deliberately low to account for inaccuracies in bounding boxes in the ground truth data, for example defining the bounding box for a highly non-convex object, e.g. a person with arms and legs spread, is somewhat subjective."* — [Everingham et al., IJCV 2010](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/pubs/everingham10.pdf)

### Inferences
- VOC's rule is "outermost visible pixel", with one exception for thin protrusions (<5% of pixels). The exception lets the box be slightly *smaller* than the object. VOC gives no allowance for making the box *larger* than the object.
- VOC treats motion blur at the image level (mark the image bad or the object difficult). Its segmentation mixed-pixel rule ("belongs to the object whose colour contributes most") is the closest official guidance on where a blurred edge lies. Applied to boxes, it would put the box edge at roughly the 50% colour transition of the blur, not at the outer tail of the blur.
- VOC's "close-fitting occluder is part of the object" rule is the reverse of amodal estimation. It enlarges the object only when the occluder hugs it (clothing, mud, snow). It does not cover a hand that happens to be in front of the object.

### Gaps
- No VOC document gives a pixel tolerance for box edges (the 5-pixel figure is for segmentation masks only). VOC also publishes no inter-annotator box IoU of its own; the 88% agreement figure in §4 comes from Papadopoulos et al. 2017.
- VOC says nothing about shadows or glow.

## 2. ImageNet / ILSVRC bounding boxes (Su, Deng, Fei-Fei 2012; Russakovsky et al. 2015)

### Takeaway
ImageNet sets the strictest definition of all the sources: the box must be *"the smallest among all bounding boxes that contain the object"*, *"Include all visible part and draw as tightly as possible."* ILSVRC explicitly rejects amodal (estimated full-extent) boxes because they are *"inherently ambiguous and ill-defined"*. It also puts a number on human box error: about **5 px per side**. The ILSVRC evaluation threshold for small objects was loosened because of that error.

### Cited Findings
- Requirement: *"Quality. Each bounding box needs to be tight, i.e. the smallest among all bounding boxes that contain the object. This would greatly facilitate the learning algorithms for the object detector by giving better alignment of the object instances"* — [Su, Deng, Fei-Fei, "Crowdsourcing Annotations for Visual Object Detection", AAAI HCOMP workshop 2012](http://vision.stanford.edu/pdf/bbox_submission.pdf)
- Drawing-task rules: *"Rule 1: Include all visible part and draw as tightly as possible."* (Rules 2–4 cover one instance per box, not re-drawing, and the no-object checkbox.) The verification task uses the same rule: *"Rule 2: A good bounding box must include all visible parts and be as tight as possible."* — [Su et al. 2012](http://vision.stanford.edu/pdf/bbox_submission.pdf)
- Strict training bar: *"Note that we set a rather high standard for getting tight bounding boxes."* Feedback message type 1 is *"the bounding box is not sufficiently tight"*. Figure 4 example: *"The worker draws the green rectangle, but it is not tight enough—the paw is outside the bounding box."* — [Su et al. 2012](http://vision.stanford.edu/pdf/bbox_submission.pdf)
- Quality control is done by a separate binary verification task with embedded gold-standard images. *"validation images with bad bounding boxes can be generated by perturbing good bounding boxes"* — [Su et al. 2012](http://vision.stanford.edu/pdf/bbox_submission.pdf)
- Results: *"99.2% of all bounding boxes are accurate (the bounding boxes are visibly tight). The remaining 0.8% are somewhat off. No bounding boxes are found to have less than 50% overlap with ground truth."* Missing boxes (2.1% of images) were *"all difficult cases—the size is too small, the boundary is blurry, or there is strong shadow."* — [Su et al. 2012](http://vision.stanford.edu/pdf/bbox_submission.pdf)
- ILSVRC rule and rationale for modal boxes: *"Every bounding box is required to be as small as possible while including all visible parts of the object instance. An alternate annotation procedure could be to annotate the full (estimated) extent of the object: e.g., if a person's legs are occluded and only the torso is visible, the bounding box could be drawn to include the likely location of the legs. However, this alternative procedure is inherently ambiguous and ill-defined, leading to disagreement among annotators and among researchers (what is the true "most likely" extent of this object?). We follow the standard protocol of only annotating visible object parts (Russell et al., 2007; Everingham et al., 2010)."* — [Russakovsky et al., ILSVRC, IJCV 2015 (arXiv 1409.0575)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0575)
- ILSVRC does not record truncation: *"We chose not to provide this level of detail in favor of annotating more images and more object instances."* — [Russakovsky et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0575)
- Quantified human box error and small-object evaluation: *"for small objects even deviations of a few pixels would be unacceptable according to this threshold. For example, consider an object B of size 10 × 10 pixels, with a detection window of 20 × 20 pixels which fully contains that object. This would be an error of approximately 5 pixels on each dimension, which is average human annotation error. However, the IOU in this case would be 100/400 = 0.25"*. Therefore *"for smaller objects we loosen the threshold in ILSVRC to allow for the annotation to extend up to 5 pixels on average in each direction around the object"*: thr(B) = min(0.5, wh/((w+10)(h+10))). *"In practice, this changes the threshold only on objects which are smaller than approximately 25 × 25 pixels, and affects 5.5% of objects in the detection validation set."* — [Russakovsky et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0575)
- ILSVRC also stresses small objects: *"since ILSVRC contains ... "nail" and "ping pong ball" which have many very small instances, it is important to include even very small object instances in evaluation."* — [Russakovsky et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0575)

### Inferences
- The ILSVRC loosened threshold is an *evaluation* allowance for about 5 px of annotation noise per side. It is **not** an instruction to add 5 px of padding; the annotation rule itself stays "as small as possible". For objects about 20–25 px wide, 5 px per side is a large fraction of the box. This is the main official evidence that human boxes on small objects are noisy at the few-pixel level.
- Blurry boundaries and strong shadows are named explicitly as the cases where the ImageNet pipeline failed to produce boxes. They were treated as "difficult", not given a special drawing rule.

### Gaps
- Su et al. give no numeric IoU acceptance threshold for the verification task; "good vs. bad" is judged visually against the rules. Neither paper says how to draw a box on a blurry edge.

## 3. MS COCO (and LVIS): boxes derived from instance masks

### Takeaway
COCO boxes are not hand-drawn rectangles. They are the enclosing box of the crowd-sourced instance **segmentation** (polygon), which covers the visible part only (an occluded object *"may require multiple polygons"*). So COCO box tightness equals mask accuracy, and COCO masks are measurably coarse: mean mask IoU with experts is 0.83–0.87, against 0.91–0.95 between two experts, per LVIS.

### Cited Findings
- COCO paper: *"we take a subset of 55,000 images from our dataset and obtain tight-fitting bounding boxes from the annotated segmentation masks."* — [Lin et al., "Microsoft COCO", ECCV 2014 (arXiv 1405.0312)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0312)
- Data format: *"Note that a single object (iscrowd=0) may require multiple polygons, for example if occluded. Crowd annotations (iscrowd=1) are used to label large groups of objects (e.g. a crowd of people). In addition, an enclosing bounding box is provided for each object (box coordinates are measured from the top left image corner and are 0-indexed)."* — [COCO data format page](https://cocodataset.org/#format-data) (fetched from `cocodataset.org/dataset/format-data.htm`)
- Object size is defined by the mask: *"approximately 41% of objects are small (area < 32²) ... Area is measured as the number of pixels in the segmentation mask."* — [COCO detection evaluation page](https://cocodataset.org/#detection-eval)
- Segmentation training and verification: *"most workers produced only coarse instance outlines. As a consequence, we required all workers to complete a training task for each object category ... Workers could not complete the task until their segmentation adequately matched the ground truth ... (approximately 1 in 3 workers passed the training stage)"*. Verification: *"Multiple workers (3 to 5) were asked to judge each segmentation and indicate whether it matched the instance well or not."* Appendix: *"Four of the 64 segmentation are known to be bad; a worker must identify 3 of the 4 known bad segmentations ... any segmentation that doesn't receive at least 4 of 5 favorable votes is discarded"*. The interface *"added zoom-in functionality to allow for efficient annotation of small objects and curved boundaries."* — [Lin et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0312)
- Crowds: *"After 10-15 instances of a category were segmented in an image, the remaining instances were marked as "crowds" ... areas marked as crowds will be ignored and not affect a detector's score."* — [Lin et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0312)
- COCO's own caution on boxes: *"Bounding boxes are a particularly crude approximation for articulated objects; in this case, the majority of the pixels in the (blue) tight-fitting bounding-box do not lie on the object."* — [Lin et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0312)
- Mask quality measured by LVIS (Table 1, 95% bootstrapped CIs, mask IoU mean / median): COCO dataset vs. experts **0.83–0.87 / 0.88–0.91**; COCO expert1 vs. expert2 **0.91–0.95 / 0.96–0.98**; LVIS dataset vs. experts **0.90–0.92 / 0.94–0.96**; LVIS expert vs. expert **0.93–0.96 / 0.96–0.98**. Boundary quality (mean): COCO dataset vs. experts 0.77–0.82. LVIS text: *"relatively coarse masks, such as those in the COCO dataset, limit the ability to differentiate algorithm-predicted mask quality beyond a certain, coarse point."* LVIS repeatability: *"Masks from the runs are consistent with a dataset average IoU of 0.85."* LVIS acceptance: *"only accept a segmentation if 4 annotators agree it is high-quality."* — [Gupta, Dollár, Girshick, "LVIS", CVPR 2019 (arXiv 1908.03195)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03195)

### Inferences
- A COCO box sits exactly on the outermost pixels *of the polygon*. Crowd polygons tend to be coarse, so a COCO box can be either slightly loose or slightly tight against the true object edge. No margin is added on purpose.
- Mask-derived boxes are **modal**: the box covers only the visible fragments, including fragments split apart by an occluder.

### Gaps
- The COCO paper and site publish no worker instruction text about boundaries (e.g. on blur or shadows) beyond the training/verification procedure. The LVIS paper does not say in its text how LVIS boxes are computed; that they are mask-enclosing boxes like COCO's is my assumption, not verified.

## 4. Open Images (V4+): "perfect box" definition, extreme clicking, verification thresholds

### Takeaway
Open Images defines a *"perfect box"* as *"the smallest possible box that contains all visible parts of the object"*. 90% of its boxes were made with extreme clicking: four clicks on the **top, bottom, left-most and right-most points of the visible object**, so by construction the box edges lie on the object's outermost visible pixels. Quality bars: annotators had to reach IoU > 0.84 against ground truth to qualify. Re-drawn boxes agree at 0.87 mean IoU. Verified (semi-automatic) boxes were accepted at IoU > 0.7 against an imagined perfect box and measure 0.77 mean.

### Cited Findings
- Definition: *"As instruction, our annotators were given the following general definition: Given a target class, a perfect box is the smallest possible box that contains all visible parts of the object (Figure 6 left). While this definition seems simple enough at first sight, there are quite a few class-dependent corner cases such as: are straps part of a camera?, is water part of a fountain?"* Consistency measures: *"we manually annotated a perfect bounding box on two examples for each of the 600 object classes. Additionally, for 20% of the classes we identified common mistakes in pilot studies. Annotators always worked on a single class at a time, and were shown the positive examples and common mistakes directly before starting each annotation session"* — [Kuznetsova et al., "The Open Images Dataset V4", IJCV 2020 (arXiv 1811.00982)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00982)
- Extreme clicking instruction: *"They are instructed to click on four extreme points (top, bottom, left-most, right-most) on the visible part of any object of this class."* Rationale: *"Extreme points are not imaginary, but are well-defined physical points on the object, which makes them easy to locate."* The paper criticises traditional drawing for *"clicking on imaginary corners of a tight box around the object. This is difficult as these corners are often outside the actual object and several adjustments are required to obtain a tight box."* — [Papadopoulos, Uijlings, Keller, Ferrari, "Extreme clicking for efficient object annotation", ICCV 2017 (arXiv 1708.02750)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02750)
- Click tolerance used to grade annotators during qualification: *"we find the pixels with the highest y-coordinate in it [the GT mask] ... Then, we select all pixels in the mask with y-coordinates within 10 pixels of any of these top pixels ... Finally, we also include in the accepted area all image pixels within 10 pixels of any of the selected pixels in the segmentation mask ... Thus the accepted area includes all top pixels in the mask, plus a tolerance region around them, both inside and outside the mask."* Pass rule: *"if all their clicks on all 5 qualification images are inside the accepted areas."* Quality control: one golden image per 10-image batch — [Papadopoulos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02750)
- Agreement numbers: *"Comparing GT Boxes and GT SegBoxes reveals this agreement to be at 88% mIoU on VOC 2007. Moreover, 93% of all GT Boxes have IoU > 0.7 with their corresponding GT SegBox."* Extreme-click boxes on VOC 2007 vs. GT SegBoxes: mIoU 88, IoU>0.7 = 92%, IoU>0.5 = 98% (GT Boxes: 88 / 93 / 98). Calibration: *"if we perturb each of the four coordinates of the GT Boxes by 4 pixels, the resulting boxes also have 88% mIoU with the unperturbed annotations."* Detectors trained on either set performed *"identical"* — [Papadopoulos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02750)
- Open Images qualification: *"They are asked to draw 800 boxes and pass if their intersection-over-union (IoU) with the ground truth is higher than 0.84 and drawing time per box is 20 seconds or less. This sets high-quality standards, as the human expert agreement is 0.88 (Papadopoulos et al., 2017)."* — [Kuznetsova et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00982)
- Box verification series acceptance: *"we instruct annotators to accept a box if its IoU with an imaginary perfect box is greater than t = 0.7 (instead of 0.5 in (Papadopoulos et al., 2016))."* During verifier training *"we ignored responses on borderline boxes with IoU ∈ (0.6, 0.8), as these are too difficult to verify."* Before training, *"only 95% of boxes with very high overlap were accepted (IoU ∈ [0.8, 0.9])"*. After three training rounds the acceptance rate for IoU ∈ [0.3, 0.6] was *"nicely below 4%"* and IoU ∈ [0.8, 0.9] boxes were accepted 98% of the time — [Kuznetsova et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00982)
- Geometric agreement: *"We randomly selected 50,000 boxes produced with extreme clicking, and had annotators redraw the box (without seeing the original box) ... We found this to be 0.87, which is very close to the human agreement of 0.88 IoU on PASCAL ... The slight difference is mainly caused by objects being generally smaller in Open Images."* Verified boxes vs. redrawn: *"We found this to be 0.77 on average ... 0.77 is widely considered as a good geometric accuracy (e.g. the COCO Challenge calls IoU > 0.75 a "strict" evaluation criterion)."* Fig. 27 caption: *"the verified boxes cover the object very well, but not perfectly."* — [Kuznetsova et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00982)
- Expert audit: *"We had a human expert examine 100 images for each of the first 150 boxable classes ... more than 26,000 boxes ... Both precision and recall are very high at 97.7% and 98.2%"* — [Kuznetsova et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00982)
- Attributes: *"GroupOf: the box covers more than 5 instances of the same class which heavily occlude each other. Partially occluded: the object is occluded by another object in the image. Truncated: the object extends outside of the image. Depiction: ... cartoon, drawing, or statue. Inside: the box captures the inside of an object"* — [Kuznetsova et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00982)
- Official site: *"These boxes have been human verified to have IoU>0.7 with a perfect box on the object, and in practice they are accurate (mean IoU ~0.77, see Sect. 4.2 of [3])."* For the segmentation-mask subset, instances were annotated only if *"the object boundaries can be confidently determined by the annotator (e.g. blurry or very dark instances are skipped)"* and the object is *"larger than 40x80 or 80x40 pixels"* — [Open Images V7 facts & figures](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/factsfigures_v7.html)
- The earlier box-verification paper used the PASCAL criterion: *"If IoU(lgt, ld) ≥ 0.5, the detection is considered correct"*. It found *"humans tend to be slightly more lenient than the IoU> 0.5 criterion"* — [Papadopoulos et al., "We don't need no bounding-boxes", CVPR 2016 (arXiv 1602.08405)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.08405)

### Inferences
- Extreme clicking fixes each box edge to a physical pixel on the visible object. That is the closest any major dataset comes to an operational definition of "exactly on the outermost visible pixels".
- The 4-px calibration is a useful rule of thumb. Moving every side of a PASCAL-sized box by 4 px yields 88% mIoU, which equals expert-vs-expert agreement. For small objects (tens of px), the same pixel error costs far more IoU, which matches Open Images' remark that its lower agreement comes from smaller objects.

### Gaps
- None of the Open Images or extreme-clicking documents gives an explicit rule for blurred edges, shadows or glow in boxes. Blur is mentioned only as a skip criterion for masks.

## 5. Driving and pedestrian datasets: KITTI, Cityscapes/CityPersons, BDD100K, Waymo, nuScenes/nuImages

### Takeaway
These datasets split into two camps. **Visible-only (modal) 2D boxes**: Waymo camera boxes, BDD100K, Cityscapes-derived visible boxes. **Amodal (full-extent) 2D boxes**: nuImages ("best guess" of the occluded part), KITTI (2D boxes linked to 3D-box projections), Waymo's optional projected 3D boxes, and CityPersons/Caltech full-body boxes. Where a dataset draws visible boxes, the wording is always "as tight as possible". At the image border, boxes stop at the border and a truncation flag or value is recorded.

### Cited Findings
- **Waymo (2D camera spec)**: *"2D bounding boxes are drawn as tightly as possible around objects in the camera images, and capture all visible parts of the object."*; *"The boxes only capture the visible parts of the object, and do not try to estimate the extent of a partially occluded object."*; *"Parts of the autonomous vehicle that are visible in the camera image and reflections are not labeled."*; vehicle box *"includes: side mirrors, but excludes small protruding parts such as antenna, flag, trailer hitch, etc."*; *"If a vehicle has any open doors ... those are excluded from the bounding box."*; *"If it is not possible to tell by looking at the camera image whether an object is a vehicle, the object is not labeled."*; *"Mannequins, statues, billboards, posters, or reflections of people are not labeled."* The 3D spec is similar: *"3D bounding boxes are drawn as tightly as possible around objects in lidar point clouds, and capture all visible parts of the object."* — [Waymo Open Dataset labeling specifications](https://github.com/waymo-research/waymo-open-dataset/blob/master/docs/labeling_specifications.md)
- **Waymo paper**: *"2D tightly fitting bounding boxes for the camera images ... In addition, researchers can extract 2D amodal camera boxes from the 3D LiDAR boxes using our provided rolling shutter aware projection library."*; *"We have performed multiple phases of label verification to ensure a high labeling quality."* — [Sun et al., "Scalability in Perception for Autonomous Driving: Waymo Open Dataset", CVPR 2020 (arXiv 1912.04838)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.04838)
- **nuImages (2D boxes, amodal)**: *"If an object is occluded, then draw the bounding box to include the occluded part of the object according to your best guess."*; *"If an object is cut off at the edge of the image, then the bounding box should stop at the image boundary."*; *"If an object is reflected clearly in a glass window, then the reflection should be annotated."*; *"If an object has extremities, the bounding box should include **all** the extremities (exceptions are the side view mirrors and antennas of vehicles)."*; *"Only label objects if the object is clear enough to be certain of what it is. If an object is so blurry it cannot be known, do not label the object."*; *"Do not label an object if its height is less than 10 pixels."*; *"Do not label an object if its less than 20% visible, unless you can confidently tell what the object is. An object can have low visibility when it is occluded or cut off by the image. The clarity and orientation of the object does not influence its visibility."* — [nuImages annotator instructions](https://github.com/nutonomy/nuscenes-devkit/blob/master/docs/instructions_nuimages.md)
- **nuImages (instance masks, modal)**: *"Given a bounding box, outline the **visible** parts of the object enclosed within the bounding box using a polygon."*; *"There should not be a discrepancy of more than 2 pixels between the edge of the object instance and the polygon."*; *"If an object is occluded by another object whose width is less than 5 pixels (e.g. a thin fence), then the external object can be included in the polygon."*; *"If parts of an object are not visible due to lighting and / or shadow, it is best to have an educated guess on the non-visible areas of the object."* — [nuImages annotator instructions](https://github.com/nutonomy/nuscenes-devkit/blob/master/docs/instructions_nuimages.md)
- **nuScenes (3D cuboids)**: *"Cuboids must be very tight. Draw the cuboid as close as possible to the edge of the object without excluding any LIDAR points. There should be almost no visible space between the cuboid border and the closest point on the object."*; visibility is binned 0–40 / 41–60 / 61–80 / 81–100% *"visible in panoramic view of all cameras."* — [nuScenes annotator instructions](https://github.com/nutonomy/nuscenes-devkit/blob/master/docs/instructions_nuscenes.md)
- **KITTI (devkit readme)**: *"First we hired a set of annotators, to label 3D bounding boxe tracklets in point clouds. Since for a pedestrian tracklet, a single 3D bounding box tracklet (dimensions have been fixed) often fits badly, we additionally labeled the left/right boundaries of each object by making use of Mechanical Turk. We also collected labels of the object's occlusion state, and computed the object's truncation via backprojecting a car/pedestrian model into the image plane."* Fields: *"truncated: Float from 0 (non-truncated) to 1 (truncated), where truncated refers to the object leaving image boundaries"*; *"occluded: Integer (0,1,2,3) indicating occlusion state: 0 = fully visible, 1 = partly occluded 2 = largely occluded, 3 = unknown"*; *"'DontCare' labels denote regions in which objects have not been labeled, for example because they have been too far away from the laser scanner."*; *"the 2D bounding box should correspond to the projection of the 3D bounding box"* (3D task) — [KITTI object devkit readme.txt](https://s3.eu-central-1.amazonaws.com/avg-kitti/devkit_object.zip)
- **KITTI (paper)**: *"we asked the annotators to additionally mark each bounding box as either visible, semi-occluded, fully occluded or truncated."* — [Geiger et al., CVPR 2012](https://www.cvlibs.net/publications/Geiger2012CVPR.pdf)
- **KITTI (benchmark page)**: *"For cars we require an overlap of 70%, while for pedestrians and cyclists we require an overlap of 50% for a detection."* Difficulties: *"Easy: Min. bounding box height: 40 Px, Max. occlusion level: Fully visible, Max. truncation: 15 % Moderate: Min. bounding box height: 25 Px, Max. occlusion level: Partly occluded, Max. truncation: 30 % Hard: Min. bounding box height: 25 Px, Max. occlusion level: Difficult to see, Max. truncation: 50 %"*; *"for the hard evaluation ~2 % of the provided bounding boxes have not been recognized by humans"* — [KITTI 2D object benchmark](https://www.cvlibs.net/datasets/kitti/eval_object.php?obj_benchmark=2d)
- Two later dataset papers describe KITTI's 2D boxes as full-extent: CityPersons says *"Existing datasets (INRIA, Caltech, KITTI) have defined bounding boxes covering the full object extent, not just the visible area."* ([Zhang, Benenson, Schiele, CityPersons, CVPR 2017 (arXiv 1702.05693)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05693)). CrowdHuman Table 1 lists KITTI as having a Full BBox but no Visible BBox ([Shao et al., CrowdHuman (arXiv 1805.00123)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00123)). **[secondary characterisation by other dataset authors]**
- **Cityscapes**: instance polygons, labelled *"from back to front such that no object boundary was marked more than once"*; *"Distant objects are annotated first and subsequently their occluders. This ensures the boundary between these objects to be shared and consistent."* Agreement: *"30 images were finely annotated twice by different annotators ... 96 % of all pixels were assigned to the same label. Since our annotators were instructed to choose a void label if unclear ... we exclude pixels having at least one void label and recount, yielding 98 % agreement."* — [Cordts et al., Cityscapes, CVPR 2016 (arXiv 1604.01685)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01685)
- **CityPersons (built on Cityscapes)**: *"These segments indicate the visible parts of humans."*; *"the full body is annotated by drawing a line from the top of the head to the middle of two feet, and the bounding box is generated using a fixed aspect ratio (0.41) ... The visible bounding box for each instance is the tightest one fully covering the segment mask"*; reflections go to ignore regions: *"people's reflection in mirror or window, etc., and mark them as ignore regions."* — [CityPersons (arXiv 1702.05693)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.05693)
- **BDD100K**: official instruction page: *"annotate the bounding box around the object as tight as possible and only annotate visible part of the object."* The same sentence appears on the box-tracking instruction page. Occluded/truncated attributes follow the VOC definitions: truncated means *"the bounding box specified for the object does not correspond to the full extent of the object ... a car extending outside the image"*. When instance boundaries cannot be seen, a "crowd" attribute is used — [BDD100K Bounding Box Annotation instructions](https://doc.bdd100k.com/instructions/bbox.html) **[secondary: search snippet; direct fetch failed]**. The paper adds only statistics: *"Roughly half of the instances are occluded ... About 7% of the instances are truncated."* — [Yu et al., BDD100K, CVPR 2020 (arXiv 1805.04687)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.04687)

### Inferences
- The protruding-parts rule differs between datasets. Waymo *includes* side mirrors and excludes antennas. nuImages excludes both mirrors and antennas. VOC excludes thin parts under 5% (its example is an aerial). So "tight" always comes with a per-class decision about what counts as part of the object. That decision is a class definition; it is not padding.
- nuImages makes the 2D box amodal but the mask modal. That shows one dataset can use both conventions on purpose, for different outputs.

### Gaps
- I could not find KITTI's worker-facing instruction text for 2D box edges (only the readme's process description). I could not retrieve BDD100K's page directly. Only Cityscapes publishes agreement numbers (pixel-level, not box-level); none of the driving datasets reports inter-annotator box IoU.

## 6. Objects365, LVIS, CrowdHuman (and Caltech): visible vs. full boxes, extent rules

### Takeaway
CrowdHuman and Caltech are the clearest examples of **two boxes per object**: a tight visible-region box plus an amodal full-body box, where annotators must *"complete the invisible part"*. Objects365 has no pixel-tightness wording. Its "bounding-box rule" is about semantic extent: cover *"the largest bounding box which would not lead to the ambiguities of defining the object category."*

### Cited Findings
- **CrowdHuman**: *"We annotate a full bounding box of each individual exhaustively. If the individual is partly occluded, the annotator is required to complete the invisible part and draw a full bounding box."* Then: *"We crop each annotated instance from the images, and send these cropped regions for annotators to draw a visible bounding box."*; *"We further send the cropped regions to annotate a head bounding box. All the annotations are double-checked by at least one different annotator to ensure the annotation quality."* Contrast with CityPersons: *"our annotation protocol is more flexible in real world scenarios which have various human poses."*; human-like objects such as statues get a specific label and are ignored in evaluation — [Shao et al., "CrowdHuman: A Benchmark for Detecting Human in a Crowd" (arXiv 1805.00123)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.00123)
- **Caltech Pedestrian**: *"annotators mark a BB that indicates the full extent of the entire pedestrian (BB-full); for occluded pedestrians this involves estimating the location of hidden parts. In addition a second BB is used to delineate the visible region (BB-vis) ... During an occlusion event, the estimated full BB stays relatively constant while the visible BB may change rapidly. For comparison, in the PASCAL labeling scheme only the visible BB is labeled and occluded objects are marked as 'truncated'."* Evaluation: *"For ground truth, the full BB is always used for matching, not the visible BB, even for partially occluded pedestrians."* Ambiguous cases are labelled *"'Person?'"* — [Dollár et al., "Pedestrian Detection: An Evaluation of the State of the Art", PAMI 2012](https://pdollar.github.io/files/papers/DollarPAMI12peds.pdf)
- **Objects365**: *"Bounding Box Rules Due to the diversity of annotators, the annotation for the bounding-boxes might be inconsistent sometimes. We define the following rule when ambiguities exist for the bounding-box. The annotator is required to cover the largest bounding box which would not lead to the ambiguities of defining the object category. For example, we need to include the decoration part of the clock ... For the right figure of Figure 4, the annotators are required to label the small bounding box because the external area of the clock will lead to another category as "tower"."* Quality: three roles (*"annotators, inspectors, examiners"*). On 200 re-annotated images (3,250 boxes), recall was 92% (COCO 83%, OpenImages 43%). *"we consider a false positive if the object category is wrong or the annotation bounding-box is not accurate. The precision of our Objects365 is obviously higher than COCO with 91.7% vs 71.9%."* — [Shao et al., "Objects365", ICCV 2019](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Shao_Objects365_A_Large-Scale_High-Quality_Dataset_for_Object_Detection_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)
- **LVIS**: masks (and therefore mask-level quality) are what LVIS specifies and measures; see §3 for the agreement numbers — [LVIS (arXiv 1908.03195)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03195)

### Inferences
- Where amodal boxes exist, they are used for articulated **persons**, whose hidden extent can be guessed from body structure. CityPersons even replaces free drawing with a head-to-feet line and a fixed 0.41 aspect ratio to make the guess repeatable. ILSVRC's objection that amodal extent is "ill-defined" is weakest for rigid objects of known shape, and a circular button is one. Still, every general-purpose multi-class dataset surveyed (VOC, ILSVRC, COCO, Open Images, Objects365) is modal.
- Objects365's "largest box" rule concerns which parts belong to the object (a clock's decoration). It does not permit background margin.

### Gaps
- The Objects365 paper does not say how accuracy was judged when counting an "inaccurate" box as a false positive (no IoU threshold given).

## 7. Is there official guidance that recommends padding/margin, or forbids it?

### Takeaway
None of the primary sources reviewed tells annotators to add padding or a margin around the object. All of them that address extent say the opposite: "tight", "smallest possible", "as small as possible", "almost no visible space". The only "padding" in these documents is on the **detector or evaluation** side (Caltech crops detector-output padding; ILSVRC loosens the IoU threshold for small objects). Neither is an annotation instruction.

### Cited Findings
- A keyword scan (`padd*`, `margin`, `loose`) of all 23 source texts found no annotation instruction to pad. The hits were about detectors or evaluation (below) or unrelated usage (e.g. "by a large margin") — scan of the documents cited in §1–6.
- Caltech: *"Some detectors output BBs with padding around the pedestrian (e.g. HOG outputs 128 × 64 BBs around 96 pixel tall people), such padding is cropped"* and *"Pedestrian model height is typically around 96-100 pixels (the size of pre-cropped pedestrians in the INRIA dataset), with an additional 28-32 pixels of padding."* Both concern detector windows, not ground truth — [Dollár et al. 2012](https://pdollar.github.io/files/papers/DollarPAMI12peds.pdf)
- ILSVRC's small-object evaluation allowance *"to allow for the annotation to extend up to 5 pixels on average in each direction around the object"* tolerates annotation noise at evaluation time — [Russakovsky et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0575)
- Wording closest to an explicit ban on margin: nuScenes *"There should be almost no visible space between the cuboid border and the closest point on the object"* ([nuScenes instructions](https://github.com/nutonomy/nuscenes-devkit/blob/master/docs/instructions_nuscenes.md)). Su et al.: *"the smallest among all bounding boxes that contain the object"* ([Su et al. 2012](http://vision.stanford.edu/pdf/bbox_submission.pdf)). Open Images: *"the smallest possible box that contains all visible parts"* ([Kuznetsova et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00982)).
- The extreme-clicking paper reports that one faster protocol's official instructions *"show an example box which is not tight around the object"*, and counts this as *"lower-quality boxes"* — [Papadopoulos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02750)

### Inferences
- The consensus across ten-plus major datasets is zero intended margin, with the box edge on the outermost visible object pixel. Human noise of a few pixels (about 4–5 px per side, measured) is tolerated in evaluation, not built into the labels.

### Gaps
- I did not survey commercial labeling-vendor guides; some of them reportedly recommend a 1–2 px margin, but that is unverified and outside these primary sources.

## 8. Hard edge cases across datasets: blur, occlusion (modal vs. amodal), truncation, shadows/reflections/glow, very small objects

### Takeaway
**Blur**: datasets tell annotators to skip or flag objects that are too blurry to recognise (VOC "bad"/"difficult", nuImages "do not label", Open Images masks skip blurry boundaries). The only rule for where a blurred *edge* lies is VOC's segmentation rule: a mixed pixel belongs to whichever colour dominates it. **Occlusion**: general-purpose datasets are modal. Amodal boxes appear in person-, driving- and 3D-oriented datasets. **Truncation**: every dataset stops the box at the image edge and records a flag or fraction. **Reflections**: rules conflict (VOC and Waymo exclude or treat as occlusion; nuImages annotates clear reflections). **Shadows and glow**: effectively unaddressed for boxes. **Small objects**: minimum-size cutoffs (nuImages 10 px height; KITTI evaluation 25/40 px) or annotator discretion (VOC), and a documented human error of about 5 px.

### Cited Findings
- Blurred edges (mask rule): *"Pixels which are mixed e.g. due to transparency, motion blur or the presence of a border should be considered to belong to the object whose colour contributes most to the mix."* — [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- Blur as a skip criterion: *"If an object is so blurry it cannot be known, do not label the object."* — [nuImages instructions](https://github.com/nutonomy/nuscenes-devkit/blob/master/docs/instructions_nuimages.md). *"the object boundaries can be confidently determined by the annotator (e.g. blurry or very dark instances are skipped)"* (masks) — [Open Images V7](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/factsfigures_v7.html). *"Images which are poor quality (e.g. excessive motion blur) should be marked bad."* — [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html). Motion blur is one criterion for "difficult" — [Everingham et al. 2010](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/pubs/everingham10.pdf)
- Blur and shadow as the documented failure modes: missing boxes were cases where *"the size is too small, the boundary is blurry, or there is strong shadow."* — [Su et al. 2012](http://vision.stanford.edu/pdf/bbox_submission.pdf)
- Occlusion, modal: VOC *"not the estimated total extent"*; ILSVRC *"only annotating visible object parts"*; Waymo *"do not try to estimate the extent of a partially occluded object"*; BDD100K *"only annotate visible part"*; Open Images *"all visible parts"*; COCO multiple polygons *"if occluded"* — sources in §1–5.
- Occlusion, amodal: nuImages *"include the occluded part of the object according to your best guess"*; CrowdHuman *"complete the invisible part"*; Caltech *"estimating the location of hidden parts"*; CityPersons *"amodal bounding box"* via head-to-feet line; Waymo optional projected *"2D amodal camera boxes"* — sources in §5–6.
- Minimum visibility to label: VOC *"less than 10-20% of the object is visible, such that you cannot be sure what class it is"* (skip); nuImages *"less than 20% visible, unless you can confidently tell what the object is"* (skip); Waymo *"at least partially visible"* and recognisable — sources in §1 and §5.
- Truncation at the image border: nuImages *"the bounding box should stop at the image boundary"*; VOC Truncated flag (>15–20% outside the box); KITTI truncation float 0–1; Open Images *"Truncated: the object extends outside of the image"* — sources in §1, §4, §5.
- Reflections and mirrors: VOC *"treat reflections on the glass as occlusion"*, *"Do label objects in mirrors"*; Waymo *"reflections are not labeled"*; nuImages *"If an object is reflected clearly in a glass window, then the reflection should be annotated"*; CityPersons reflections are ignore regions — sources in §1, §5.
- Shadows: the only explicit rule found is nuImages (masks), which estimates object parts hidden by shadow: *"If parts of an object are not visible due to lighting and / or shadow, it is best to have an educated guess on the non-visible areas of the object."* VOC says silhouettes/poor illumination should not be marked poor quality *"unless objects cannot be recognised"* — [nuImages](https://github.com/nutonomy/nuscenes-devkit/blob/master/docs/instructions_nuimages.md), [VOC2012](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- Thin occluders and thin parts: VOC close-fitting occluders (clothing/mud/snow) count as part of the object; one-pixel structures may be ignored in masks. nuImages: occluders narrower than 5 px may be included in the polygon — sources in §1, §5.
- Small objects: VOC *"the object is very small (at your discretion)"*; nuImages *"Do not label an object if its height is less than 10 pixels."*; KITTI evaluation minimum height 25 px (Moderate/Hard) and 40 px (Easy); ILSVRC includes *"even very small object instances"* and measures *"approximately 5 pixels on each dimension ... average human annotation error"*; Open Images attributes its lower agreement (0.87 vs 0.88) to *"objects being generally smaller"*; COCO added *"zoom-in functionality to allow for efficient annotation of small objects"* — sources in §1–5.

### Inferences
- For motion-blurred edges, the official guidance supports putting the box edge where the object's colour still dominates (about the mid-transition of the blur), not at the faint outer tail and not with a safety margin. This rests on VOC's segmentation rule plus the universal "tight" wording.
- A cast shadow, glow or halo is not part of the object under any "visible parts of the object" definition. No dataset explicitly says to include one. Su et al. list "strong shadow" only as a difficulty.

### Gaps
- No primary source addresses **glow, halo or light bleed** (e.g. an illuminated button or LED) or **JPEG ringing** at box edges.
- No source publishes box-level inter-annotator IoU specifically for very small objects (under about 30 px), although ILSVRC's 5-px error estimate and Open Images' size remark imply agreement drops.

## 9. How annotation quality is verified: agreement numbers and acceptance thresholds

### Takeaway
The benchmark figures are: human expert box agreement of about **0.87–0.88 mean IoU** (PASCAL, Open Images); verified-box acceptance at **IoU > 0.7**; annotator qualification at **IoU > 0.84**; evaluation matching at IoU 0.5 (VOC, KITTI pedestrians), 0.7 (KITTI cars) and 0.75 ("strict", COCO). Human box error is roughly **4–5 px per side**.

### Cited Findings
- PASCAL expert agreement 88% mIoU; 93% of GT boxes at IoU > 0.7 against boxes fit to GT masks; ±4 px perturbation of every coordinate gives 88% mIoU — [Papadopoulos et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.02750)
- Open Images: qualification IoU > 0.84 on 800 boxes at ≤ 20 s per box; redraw agreement 0.87 (50,000 boxes); verification acceptance IoU > 0.7, measured 0.77 mean; expert audit precision 97.7% and recall 98.2% (> 26,000 boxes) — [Kuznetsova et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00982); [Open Images V7](https://storage.googleapis.com/openimages/web/factsfigures_v7.html)
- ImageNet: 99.2% of boxes "visibly tight", 0.8% "somewhat off", none below 50% overlap; about 5 px average human error per dimension; small-object threshold min(0.5, wh/((w+10)(h+10))) — [Su et al. 2012](http://vision.stanford.edu/pdf/bbox_submission.pdf); [Russakovsky et al. 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1409.0575)
- COCO: segmentation verification by 3–5 workers with at least 4 of 5 favourable votes; COCO mask vs. expert mean IoU 0.83–0.87; expert vs. expert 0.91–0.95 — [Lin et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1405.0312); [LVIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.03195)
- VOC: 50% threshold set *"deliberately low to account for inaccuracies in bounding boxes in the ground truth data"*; segmentation accuracy *"within 5 pixels"* — [Everingham et al. 2010](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/pubs/everingham10.pdf); [VOC2012 guidelines](http://host.robots.ox.ac.uk/pascal/VOC/voc2012/guidelines.html)
- nuImages masks: at most 2 px discrepancy from the object edge — [nuImages instructions](https://github.com/nutonomy/nuscenes-devkit/blob/master/docs/instructions_nuimages.md)
- Cityscapes: 96% (98% excluding void) pixel agreement on doubly annotated images — [Cordts et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01685)
- KITTI: evaluation overlap 70% for cars, 50% for pedestrians and cyclists — [KITTI 2D benchmark](https://www.cvlibs.net/datasets/kitti/eval_object.php?obj_benchmark=2d)
- Objects365: precision 91.7% vs. COCO 71.9% (inaccurate box counts as FP); three-tier annotator/inspector/examiner review — [Objects365](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2019/papers/Shao_Objects365_A_Large-Scale_High-Quality_Dataset_for_Object_Detection_ICCV_2019_paper.pdf)
- COCO calls IoU > 0.75 a "strict" criterion — [COCO detection eval](https://cocodataset.org/#detection-eval); quoted in [Kuznetsova et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00982)

### Inferences
- A fixed pixel error costs much more IoU on a small box. For a square box of side s with every side off by d px (all outward), IoU = s²/(s+2d)². With d = 4: s = 20 gives 0.51, s = 40 gives 0.69, s = 94 gives 0.85. So the human noise that is harmless on PASCAL-sized objects (about 88% mIoU at ±4 px) drags small-object boxes close to the 0.5–0.7 acceptance and evaluation thresholds. This is my own arithmetic, applying the Papadopoulos/ILSVRC error magnitudes. No source reports these numbers.

### Gaps
- Waymo, BDD100K, nuImages, CrowdHuman and KITTI describe review steps ("multiple phases of label verification", "double-checked") but publish no box IoU agreement numbers.
